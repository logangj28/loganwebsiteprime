Basic Rules of Fantasy Fooball!

How the game works

In fantasy football, you score points each week based on actions performed in real NFL games by the players on your fantasy team. For example, one rushing yard gives you 0.1 points, and a touchdown gives you 6.0. Each week you face one opponent in your league and recieve either a win or a loss based on who scored more points in their starting lineup. At the end of the season, the 4-6 teams with the most wins go to the playoffs and duke it out to see who is champion.

Drafting Rules

Teams, usually 10 or 12, will perform a snake draft of any NFL players based on their expectation of how many fantasy points they will score and the scarcity of quality scorers at their position. Building a good team is all about predicting who other players might pick and correctly assessing who your leaguemates are under or over valueing.

Reward and Punishment

Conventionally, fantasy football leagues will include a small buy in paid to the commisioner (Ex: $20). Each players buy is put into a pot which is given to the fantasy football champion.

Additionally, there must be punishment for the loser to keep people in the bottom half of the league from losing interest and colluding or quitting. The fantasy football punishment must be agreed upon by the league prior to the draft, and carried out before the start of the next season. Examples include the 24 hour Waffle House challenge and the infamous milk mile.

What You Need to Know Before Playing

Positional Values

When drafting your fantasy football team, the most obvious strategy would be to fill your starting lineup first and draft the players projected to score the most points. In this years case, this would mean taking Josh Allen first overall. However, what really matters is opportunity cost. While Allen is likely to be the best fantasy QB this year, there is a smaller spread in the amount of points QBs score than other positions, and traditionally each team is only allowed to start 1 QB. Because of this, waiting and drafting the 12th QB off the board at a much cheaper cost is often smart. In leagues where you can start 1QB, 2RBs, 2WRs, 1TE, and 1FLEX (RB/WR/TE), running backs are usually the most scarce position compared to the amount which you can start, 2-3. Their scarcity stems from the fact that you have to start multiple of them and the fact that there is a steep drop off between points scored by the best backs and points scored by mediocre RBs. This is why the most common picks in the first and second rounds have traditionally been RBs.

Trading

From the draft until the trade deadline, trading players in fantasy football is unrestricted. If you and a leaguemate disagree on the values of different players, or your teams have holes and depth at opposite positions, a trade may be beneficial for both parties. In most cases, however, trades do have a winner and a loser because fantasy is a zero sum game. Finally, you may not make trades in promise of future assets or assets which are not contained within the game of fantasy football. If this rule is broken, or if a trade is suspected to be collusion based solely on friendship, the trade may be vetoed by the commisioner or a supermajority vote from the league. Vetos are generally considered a last resort, never to be used unless true collusion is suspected.

A Game of Chance

Remember, at the end of the day fantasy football is about 30-40% skill and a majority luck based. While good management can elevate your chances of winning, bad luck can all but end your season on a dime. Because of this, never gamble money you are not willing to lose, and NEVER agree to a fantasy punishment that you would not be willing to carry out.
